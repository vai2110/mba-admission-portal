Is the MBA worth considering?

CU's University School of Business offers a conventional MBA route alongside specialised programmes in areas such as Business Analytics, Strategic HR, Banking and Financial Services, Digital Marketing and other industry-aligned domains. The right question is not simply whether the MBA is popular; it is whether the specialisation and placement ecosystem match your target role.

Use the MBA page as a career filter

Before applying, decide whether you want general management, analytics, HR, finance/BFSI, marketing or another domain. Then evaluate the curriculum and recruiter fit against that target.

How does MBA admission work?

CU's current MBA admissions route uses CUCET for applicable candidates. The university's CUCET policy identifies MBA and specialised MBA programmes as mandatory CUCET programmes, with exemptions for candidates scoring more than 90% in the qualifying examination or up to 50,000 JEE rank where applicable. The MBA admissions page describes CUCET as an eligibility and scholarship test.

What does the MBA cost?

The mandatory disclosure currently lists ₹1.69 lakh for MBA Strategic HR. Other MBA programmes and specialised routes can have different fee structures, so students should not assume one fee applies to every MBA variant.

Scholarships can materially change the effective cost. CU's 2026-27 PG early-bird policy has multiple merit slabs, including up to 100% scholarship in the highest slab during the relevant phase.

Calculate net cost, not headline fee

Subtract confirmed scholarship benefits, then add hostel/living costs and other applicable charges. Compare the resulting cost with the programme's actual placement outcomes.

What are the MBA placement signals?

The official MBA admissions page currently reports a ₹28 LPA highest package, 400+ MNCs recruiting management students and 1,427 offers made to management students. Treat the highest package as an upper-end outcome and investigate average/median and role-level data before calculating ROI.

How should you judge MBA ROI?

Strong fit

You have a clear target function, the specialisation supports it, scholarship reduces cost and recruiter roles match your goal.

Needs caution

You are choosing MBA only because you are unsure what to do after graduation and are relying on the highest package.
