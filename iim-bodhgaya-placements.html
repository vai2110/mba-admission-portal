What are the latest placement numbers?

The current official placement page reports 100% placement, 200+ recruiting companies, ₹45.63 lakh highest CTC and ₹14.80 LPA average CTC. These are the headline indicators students usually search for, but the average and distribution are more useful than the highest offer when you are estimating realistic ROI.

The institute also publishes annual final-placement and summer-internship reports. The newest report should always take priority over older summaries when the same metric appears in different forms.

What do the top 25%, 50% and 75% numbers tell you?

Percentile-level averages are valuable because they show how compensation changes as a larger share of the class is included. They prevent a single high-value offer from dominating the placement conversation.

Top 25%

₹21.00 LPA average. This indicates the compensation level reached by the strongest quarter of the reported cohort.

Top 50%

₹17.95 LPA average. A broader view of the class than the top-quarter figure.

Top 75%

₹16.24 LPA average. Helps a student judge the outcome beyond the highest-performing segment.

Important limitation

These are still cohort-level averages. They cannot tell you which salary you personally will receive because role, recruiter, prior experience, academics, interview performance and market conditions affect selection.

How does the placement process work?

The official process begins when a recruiter registers and submits a job description. Students then express interest in relevant roles, companies shortlist candidates, and the recruitment cycle may include a pre-placement talk, written test, case study, group discussion and personal interview before offers are rolled out.

1. Understand the role

Read the job description, eligibility rules, location, compensation and joining conditions before expressing interest.

2. Clear screening

Be prepared for aptitude tests, cases, group discussions or role-specific screening where applicable.

3. Convert the interview

Build a coherent career story and prepare for HR, behavioural and technical/business questions relevant to the role.

The institute notes that students who register for a particular job and submit their names/CVs may not be allowed to withdraw from that recruitment process. Students should therefore treat EOI decisions seriously.

What about summer internships?

The Summer Internship Programme gives first-year PGP students industry exposure before final placements. The 2024-26 official SIP report records 100% SIP placements and participation from more than 100 companies for MBA and MBA-DBM, with opportunities across BFSI, IT & Analytics, consulting, FinTech, FMCG, e-commerce, healthcare and manufacturing.

The SIP matters because the internship can become a bridge between classroom learning and the full-time role a student later targets. When comparing schools, examine both final placements and internship quality rather than looking only at the final CTC.

How should you prepare for placements?

Before the first shortlist

Build a clean one-page CV, know your academic/work story, strengthen aptitude and track the sectors you want.

For consulting & analytics

Practise cases, structured problem solving, Excel/data interpretation and concise communication.

For BFSI & finance

Revise accounting, financial statements, valuation, markets, banking and role-specific current affairs.

For marketing & general management

Prepare market-sizing, consumer thinking, business awareness, sales scenarios and behavioural questions.
