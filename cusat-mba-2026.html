CUSAT MBA Selection Process: 70% Test + 20% PI + 10% Class XII

SMS states that applicants need a bachelor's degree from a recognised university and must meet the minimum qualifying marks prescribed by CUSAT norms. Accepted management aptitude scores for the regular MBA include CAT, CMAT and KMAT.

For the Executive MBA, relevant work experience is mandatory under the prospectus rules. Candidates without an accepted test score can use the SMS Departmental Admission Test where permitted.

CUSAT MBA Fees 2026: Tuition and Other Charges

CUSAT SMS also offers an International Dual Masters Programme in Management. The official programme page describes it as a two-year route where students complete the first year at CUSAT and progress to a partner university abroad for the second year.

This is a materially different financial and career decision from the regular MBA because overseas tuition, living costs, visa and travel expenses must be evaluated separately.

CUSAT MBA FAQs: Fees, Entrance Exams, Seats & Selection

For an MBA applicant, the relevant comparison is between total education cost, placement role quality, recruiter participation, internship access and the candidate's entrance score. A low fee does not by itself guarantee a high ROI.

Use this calculation

Total cost = academic fees + hostel/living + travel + opportunity cost. Then compare that against realistic post-MBA roles and not the highest package quoted by any one source.

CUSAT MBA FAQs

What exams are accepted for CUSAT MBA?

SMS accepts CAT, CMAT and KMAT for the regular MBA programmes in the 2026 admission process.

What is the CUSAT MBA selection weightage?

The final rank is based on 70% management aptitude test score, 20% personal interview and 10% Class XII marks.

How many seats are there in CUSAT MBA?

The admissions portal lists 120 seats for MBA Full Time, 30 for MBA Evening Regular and 60 for MBA Executive.

Is CUSAT MBA worth it?

It can be attractive for students prioritising a public-university fee structure, campus MBA and a competitive entrance-based selection process. The final decision should use current placement and total-cost evidence.
