What do the headline placement numbers actually mean?

CU reports 10,000+ placement offers from 1,300+ companies for batch 2024-25. It also reports a ₹59.9 LPA highest national package and ₹1.7 crore highest international package. These are useful indicators of recruiter scale and upper-end outcomes, but they should not be mistaken for an average salary or an individual student's expected package.

One important distinction: offers are not necessarily unique students

CU also highlights multiple offers, so 10,000+ offers should be read as an offer-volume metric. For ROI, compare programme-specific average/median outcomes wherever available.

How should you read the salary bands?

The official placement page reports 20 LPA packages from 40+ companies, 15 LPA from 50+ companies, 10 LPA from 100+ companies, 7 LPA from 350+ companies and 5 LPA from 650+ companies. This distribution is more informative than focusing only on the highest package because it shows the number of companies at different salary thresholds.

₹20 LPA+

40+ companies reported. This is a high-end band and will be relevant to a smaller part of the cohort.

₹10 LPA+

100+ companies reported. A useful benchmark for evaluating stronger recruiter opportunities.

₹5 LPA+

650+ companies reported. This gives a broader view of recruiter salary coverage.

Which recruiters visit the campus?

CU's placement pages name companies such as Microsoft, Amazon, IBM, SAP Labs, Hitachi, Deloitte, EY, Flipkart, HP, TAFE, John Deere, Schindler, Mindtree, Taj, Oberoi, Hyatt, Vistara and others. Recruiter relevance still depends on the programme and eligibility criteria for each drive.

Recruiter count is not the same as recruiter access

A large campus can host many recruiters while individual programme cohorts see a narrower set. Students should therefore ask which companies recruited their specific institute, branch or programme.

Why programme-level context matters

Engineering, management, computing, business and other programmes have different recruiter pools and selection criteria. For example, the university's Joint Placement Programme publishes drives with branch-specific eligibility such as B.Tech Mechanical, Civil, Electrical and other disciplines.

B.Tech

Expect technical assessments, coding or core-engineering tests depending on branch and employer.

MBA

Expect aptitude, communication, case, interview and business-domain assessment depending on role.

BCA

Technical skills, projects, coding and application-development capability can materially influence outcomes.

BBA

Communication, business understanding, analytics and role-specific functional skills become important.

How should students prepare before placements?

Placement outcomes are strongly affected by what a student builds before the recruitment season. The university highlights practical learning and industry exposure; students should turn that into evidence through projects, internships, certifications where relevant, aptitude practice and interview preparation.

StageStudent action
Year 1Build fundamentals, communication and basic technical/business skills.
Year 2Complete meaningful projects and pursue internships aligned to your target role.
Final yearPrepare aptitude/coding/domain tests, resume, interviews and role-specific cases.

Is the placement record enough to choose CU?

No. It is one of the decision inputs. A sensible comparison should combine programme fee, scholarship, curriculum, student intake, location, internship opportunities, average/median salary where available, recruiter fit and your own career target.
