Rajagiri Business School Placements: latest reported outcome

Quick answer

The latest NIRF 2026 data for students graduating in 2024-25 reports 201 students placed out of 215 graduates, with a ₹7.50 LPA median salary and 8 students opting for higher studies.

A separate RBS placement report for the 2023-25 batch reports 272 students opting for placements, 249 placed, 170 companies/recruiters, top-10 placements of ₹16.13 LPA, top-50 of ₹11.26 LPA, top-100 of ₹9.28 LPA and 21 international placements.

The two datasets use different batch/reporting frameworks. They are shown separately rather than being merged into one placement percentage.

Which companies recruit from Rajagiri Business School?

Recent recruiter lists include ICICI Bank, Nestle, KPMG, ITC, Godrej, Havells, HDFC Bank, Coca-Cola, EY, Goldman Sachs, Federal Bank and PwC. Recruiter participation varies by year, so a company appearing on a recruiter list does not guarantee that every specialisation or student will receive an offer from that company.

How does RBS support placements?

The Office of Corporate Relations coordinates final placements, summer internships, industry interactions, professional-body associations and career support. RBS also states that the placement function supports career counselling and pre-placement training and maintains relationships with recruiters.

The career counselling function helps students choose electives/specialisations, evaluate career paths and understand industry options, including international placements and higher studies.

RBS Placement ROI

With current PGDM tuition reported at ₹7.50 lakh and the latest NIRF median salary at ₹7.50 LPA, the tuition-to-median comparison is relatively straightforward. But this does not mean the programme pays back in one year.

Positive factors

Moderate tuition, scholarship options, 201 placements in the latest NIRF cohort and a broad recruiter base.

Check before deciding

Role quality, fixed vs variable pay, location, hostel/living expenses, scholarship and loan interest.
