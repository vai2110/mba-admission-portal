How CollegeDecoded researches and verifies college information

This working draft explains the editorial checks proposed for college profiles, admissions pages, fee guides, cutoff pages and placement reports. Please review and approve it before the page is made indexable or linked from the main navigation.

1. Source hierarchy

College-specific facts should be checked against the institution's own website, current admission brochure, official fee notice, programme page, placement report or signed notice. Regulatory and ranking information should be checked against the relevant official authority, such as UGC, AICTE, NAAC or NIRF. Entrance-exam information should come from the official exam-conducting body.

Third-party college directories, coaching websites, aggregator pages and unsourced social posts are not acceptable evidence for fees, cutoffs, admission dates, rankings or placement statistics.

2. How figures are reported

  • Every fee, cutoff, seat count, ranking, package or admission date should carry the academic year or batch to which it applies.
  • Average, median and highest salary figures must remain separate. They must not be presented as interchangeable measures.
  • Placement percentages should be shown only when the source explains the denominator and reporting batch.
  • Cutoffs should identify the exam, category, quota, round and programme wherever the official source provides those details.
  • If the institute has not published a figure, the page should say that it is not published. Do not estimate or fill the gap.

3. Dates and freshness

Past deadlines must never be described as upcoming. A future date should be published only when an official notice confirms it. If the next cycle has not been announced, say that the date is not yet announced and link to the official admissions page.

The Last updated date should change only when the page receives a substantive editorial or factual update. The Data as of label should identify the academic year or batch supported by the sources, not simply the current calendar year.

4. Placement and fee comparisons

Any fee-versus-salary comparison is an editorial calculation, not an institute-published ROI guarantee. If included, show the inputs, their source years and the formula. For example: published programme fee divided by published median salary. State that this simple ratio excludes living costs, taxes, loan interest, opportunity cost and individual outcomes.

Peer comparisons should use the same metric definitions and comparable batches. If the source years differ, disclose that limitation rather than presenting the numbers as directly equivalent.

5. Student discussions and quotations

Do not invent student quotes, experiences or consensus. Community discussions may be summarized only when the original public discussion can be linked and the wording is clearly described as an individual account, not an official institute position. Remove empty discussion sections that add no verifiable student perspective.

6. Corrections and review

When an official notice changes, update the affected fact, source link, data year and Last updated date. If two official sources conflict, record the conflict and seek editorial review before changing the displayed figure.

Readers should verify the latest official notice with the institute before submitting an application or paying fees. CollegeDecoded content is informational and does not guarantee admission, scholarships or placements.
